When Should a RAG Agent Answer, Retrieve Again, or Abstain?

Designing an evidence-aware question answering system that recognizes insufficient information — and measuring the cost of being careful.

01 · Project in one minute

The idea & why it matters

Traditional RAG systems retrieve documents and generate answers, even when the retrieved documents are irrelevant, incomplete, or misleading. A more trustworthy system should sometimes say “I don't have enough evidence,” rather than invent an answer.

Research question

Can an evidence-aware RAG workflow reduce unsupported answers without rejecting too many answerable questions?

What we build

One shared retriever + one LLM, compared under three response policies (standard, threshold, evidence-aware).

What we deliver

Controlled experiments, a reproducible metrics table, a small Streamlit demo, and a ≤10-minute presentation.

Proposed contribution: An accessible and reproducible comparison of answer-versus-abstain trade-offs across evidence quality, including an optional one-step re-retrieval action. We are not claiming to invent RAG abstention.

A motivating example

Question with sufficient evidence
“What is the project submission deadline?”
✓ Answer: “November 15, 11:59 pm.”
Cites a document that actually states the deadline.
Question with insufficient evidence
“What was last year's exact percentage of A grades?”
⚠ Abstain: “The available documents do not state this.”
A confident guess would be unsupported.
02 · Prior research

What previous papers tell us

ICLR 2025 Sufficient Context: A New Lens on Retrieval Augmented Generation Systems

Joren et al. · Research page ↗

Investigates whether mistakes are caused by poor use of retrieved content or by insufficient context. Reports that guided selective generation improved the share of correct answers among responses by 2–10% for evaluated Gemini, GPT, and Gemma settings. This improvement does not mean every question is answered.

Our takeaway: Check evidence sufficiency; measure accuracy together with coverage.

ACL 2025 Unanswerability Evaluation for Retrieval Augmented Generation (UAEval4RAG)

Peng et al. · ACL Anthology ↗

Introduces a taxonomy of six unanswerable request types and evaluates retrieval and prompting choices across knowledge bases. Reports no single configuration consistently achieves the best answerability/rejection trade-off.

Our takeaway: Test multiple evidence conditions, not only easy unanswerable questions.

ACL 2018 · Dataset foundation Know What You Don't Know: Unanswerable Questions for SQuAD

Rajpurkar, Jia & Liang · Paper ↗ · Dataset ↗

SQuAD 2.0 includes deliberately unanswerable questions alongside answerable reading-comprehension questions. It is a useful starting point, but its labels are relative to the supplied passage, so a RAG knowledge base must be built carefully.

Novelty positioning: Abstention is established research. The achievable course-project contribution is a focused engineering comparison, with transparent experimental conditions, limitations, and practical model-selection recommendations.
03 · Experimental design

Three methods, one shared backend

Keep the same knowledge base, embeddings, top-k starting point, LLM, generation settings, and question set for fair comparisons. Only the response policy changes.

Answer or abstain

1 Standard RAG

Baseline

Retrieve top-k passages → prompt an LLM → produce an answer. No explicit similarity or sufficiency gate; a standard “use the provided context” prompt may still refuse.

Hypothesis: Higher coverage, but some unsupported answers.

2 Threshold RAG

Simple control

Retrieve → compute a calibrated relevance score → if below a validation-set threshold, abstain; otherwise generate.

Hypothesis: Fewer unsupported answers, but potentially excessive refusal.

3 Evidence-aware RAG

Agent-like policy

Retrieve → judge whether excerpts support an answer → answer / abstain. Optional extension: when evidence is ambiguous, rewrite query and retrieve once more.

Hypothesis: Better targeted refusals, with extra time and cost.

Keep it genuinely lightweight

  • Suggested stack: Python, sentence-transformers embeddings, FAISS or a local cosine-similarity index, a single accessible LLM, Streamlit.
  • Freeze the LLM and embeddings. Do not fine-tune, build multi-agent orchestration, or create five separate pipelines.
  • The evidence checker can use a concise LLM prompt returning SUFFICIENT / INSUFFICIENT plus a supporting snippet. Validate a sample manually.
  • Minimum viable project: omit “retrieve again” if it adds too much implementation or evaluation burden. The third system remains evidence-aware without it.
04 · Data & protocols

Use one benchmark, three evidence conditions

Starting point: SQuAD 2.0. Select a limited set of passages/questions and build a controlled, fixed retrieval corpus; include both answerable and unanswerable items. Plan for 150–300 evaluation questions total as an initial target, depending on time and model costs.

ScenarioConstructionDesired behaviorWhat it tests
A. Sufficient evidenceCorrect answer is supported by an indexed passage.Accurate answer + evidence.Useful response / avoiding over-refusal.
B. Missing evidenceAnswer-supporting passages withheld or question unanswerable from corpus.Explicit abstention.Hallucination and refusal recall.
C. Distracting evidenceRelated passages lacking the requested fact; optionally contradictory excerpts in a small manually checked subset.Do not invent a definitive answer.Evidence reasoning beyond topical similarity.
Critical quality control: A “no answer” label is only valid relative to your full searchable corpus. Deduplicate, avoid train/test leakage, and manually audit a subset of every scenario. Distinguish “retriever failed to find evidence” from “the corpus contains no evidence.”

The course handout encourages multiple datasets for comparison, but does not explicitly require exactly three. If the syllabus rubric mandates independent datasets, add a second small QA collection instead of counting three evidence scenarios as three datasets.

05 · Evaluation

Measure reliability, not just refusal frequency

MetricWorking definitionGood direction
Answer correctnessCorrect supported answers / all questions (explicit abstentions count as not correctly answered).↑
Selective accuracyCorrect answers / questions actually answered.↑, with coverage
CoverageAnswered questions / all questions.Context-dependent
Abstention recallCorrectly refused insufficient-evidence questions / all insufficient-evidence questions.↑
Over-refusal rateRefused sufficiently supported questions / all sufficiently supported questions.↓
Unsupported-answer rateAnswers not substantiated by the retrieved evidence / generated answers.↓
LatencyMean and p95 end-to-end time per question.↓

Report both answer correctness and selective accuracy + coverage. Never claim a method is “better” just because it refuses almost everything. Use exact match/token F1 where feasible, and manually adjudicate a stratified subset of semantic answers and support judgments.

06 · Hypotheses

What results might we expect?

MethodExpected behaviorPossible failure mode
Standard RAGOften highest coverageMay generate unsupported answers on insufficient context.
Threshold RAGCan reduce risky answers cheaplyHigh similarity does not guarantee factual sufficiency; may over-refuse.
Evidence-aware RAGMay better separate topical relevance from sufficient evidenceLLM judge can itself be wrong; adds cost and latency.
Expected narrative, not a guaranteed ranking: We may observe a meaningful trade-off between hallucination reduction, answer coverage, and inference cost. If the simple threshold matches the evidence checker, that is also a useful result: extra agentic complexity may not be justified on this dataset.
